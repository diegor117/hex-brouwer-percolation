Hex, Brouwer and percolation: simulations

Diego Rapini, Luigi Lanni, Massimiliano Iorio

Interactive versions of the figures in the paper The Hex theorem, Brouwer's fixed-point theorem, and a crossing probability equal to one half (PDF). The definitions and proofs are in the paper; this page only contains the simulations.

  1. Hex board
  2. Gale's walk
  3. Gale's colouring of the square
  4. Crossing probability

1. Hex board

A board for two players at one computer. Black moves first and connects the top and bottom edges; White connects the left and right edges.

2. Gale's walk (paper, Section 2)

The construction in the proof that every full board has a crossing, on a random 6 × 6 board.

3. Gale's colouring of the square (paper, Section 3)

Cells are coloured by the rule in the proof of the approximate fixed-point lemma for the chosen map f, mesh and ε. Each cell is drawn as a square centred at its point of the unit square. The ring marks the cell whose point moves least.

white: |f1(x) − x1| ≥ ε black: otherwise, |f2(x) − x2| ≥ ε uncoloured: both less than ε

4. Crossing probability (paper, Section 4)

Each cell is black with probability p, independently. The chart shows the running frequency of a black crossing. At p = 0.5 the exact probability is 1/2 on every square board.
